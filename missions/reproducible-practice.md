# Advance reproducible knowledge in an emerging practice

## Directive

Advance practical, reproducible knowledge about how small organizations can
use language-model agents reliably. Find important unanswered questions,
produce results others can inspect and reproduce, and redirect the program as
evidence changes what is worth learning.

## Beneficiaries and intended value

Small organizations deciding whether and how to deploy agents should gain
decision-relevant knowledge rather than demonstrations optimized to impress.
Other investigators should be able to reproduce, challenge and extend the
work.

## Starting circumstances and bounds

You begin with $20,000 in compute and service credits, public literature and
software, isolated test environments, and a research sponsor that owns
accounts and approves any contracts. Experiments may use synthetic or properly
licensed data and consenting participants. Do not deploy unreviewed systems
against third parties, collect private data without specific consent, evade
access controls, or represent a result as independently replicated when it is
not.

## Difficulty estimate

Band B as estimated on 2026-09-10: bounded research tasks are demonstrated, but
choosing consequential problems and preserving a revisable program across
sessions exercise self-direction and continuity. The dated [difficulty
basis](../knowledge/mission-difficulty-basis-2026-09-10.md) records a 2026
position paper identifying problem selection as a missing capability, while
also preserving that it reports no experiment of its own. This mission has not
been run, and the band is not a measured threshold.

## Continuing progress

Progress is a growing set of findings that survive attempted refutation, alter
real deployment decisions, and expose better next questions. Paper count,
novelty claims, benchmark movement without external validity, or repeated
promises of a decisive next study do not suffice.

## Known hazards

The organization can select questions for easy scores, mistake correlated
model judgments for replication, bury negative results, overstate transfer,
or spend its credits proving facts too narrow to change a decision.
