# Judge progress under a continuing mission

Reach a decision for the next observation period without pretending to settle
the mission forever. Treat the report as an index into evidence, not as the
evidence itself.

## Reconstruct the period before judging it

Start from the directive and causal account that governed the period. Follow
the report's claims into dated decisions, versioned artifacts, independent
observations and measures chosen before results were visible. Record missing
or conflicting evidence; polished prose does not fill either gap.

Separate four layers:

1. resources committed and activities performed;
2. outputs that exist and survive inspection;
3. changes for beneficiaries, including adverse and unintended effects; and
4. the organization's credible contribution to those changes, with plausible
   alternatives still visible.

Do not impose one common scale across missions. Revenue, voluntary use,
institutional change, reproducibility, beneficiary judgment and corpus
trustworthiness can each be relevant under different directives.

## Test whether the trajectory changed

Ask what uncertainty the period retired and which decision changed because of
that evidence. A changed decision is learning only when the evidence warrants
it. Compare a claimed pivot with the pre-change assumptions and dated reason:
a traceable response to evidence or changed conditions can be a pivot; a new
story attached afterward is drift.

An early loss or absent product-market fit is not decisive. Judge whether the
exposure was proportionate to a named uncertainty and whether the result made
continuation, redirection or abandonment more informed. Conversely, shipped
artifacts and growing activity are not progress when they do not change
beneficiary outcomes, retire uncertainty or support a consequential decision.

Treat measures introduced after results were known as exploratory, not as
prior success criteria. Reconsider a bad precommitted measure rather than
rewarding fidelity to an irrelevant target.

## Demand a falsifiable next commitment

Require the next commitment to name:

- an observable result and the mission-specific evidence that would count;
- a horizon covered by the next review;
- the resources and risks exposed; and
- what the organization will continue, redirect, pause or stop if the
  expectation fails.

Long-horizon work can remain credible before its ultimate outcome appears, but
only when intermediate work produces informative observations and makes the
next claim more falsifiable. Repeating the same promise without new information
is adverse evidence.

## Make a bounded decision

Decide whether to continue, redirect, pause or stop for the next period. Cite
the record behind the judgment and locate disagreement in the evidence, causal
inference, mission value or acceptable risk instead of hiding it in a weighted
score. State what would change the decision.
