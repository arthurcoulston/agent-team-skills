# Integrate expertise and disagreement

Produce one result a decision owner can use while keeping every consequential
claim traceable. Integration is not a vote and the synthesis owner is not the
source of truth: the owner is accountable for comparing the contributions,
choosing the applicable decision rule and making unresolved disagreement
visible.

## Name the synthesis owner and the decision to support

Before merging anything, name one synthesis owner. State the decision or
deliverable the combined work must support, its acceptance criteria and who
has authority to decide. The synthesis owner may also be the decision owner,
but do not infer decision authority from the assignment to synthesize.

This skill begins when contributions are available. Use the
[delegation skill](../coordinating-delegated-work/SKILL.md) to decide whether
to divide work and how to commission it. Use the
[independent-review skill](../reviewing-another-agents-work/SKILL.md) when a
contribution or final artifact needs a separate acceptance judgment.

## Integrate claims before writing unified prose

Make each consequential claim separately addressable before composing the
result. For each one, retain:

- the claim and the conditions under which it is meant to hold;
- its contributor and source or artifact revision;
- the strongest supporting and contrary evidence, with confidence and material
  limits; and
- a disposition of **adopted**, **qualified**, **rejected** or **unresolved**,
  with the reason.

Ask each contributor what its inquiry found that the others could not have
seen. Do not treat a missing answer as proof that nothing unique exists. The
ledger's purpose is to expose what the synthesis has and has not accounted
for, not to manufacture completeness.

Preserve provenance through the final artifact. A reader must be able to move
from a material conclusion back to the contribution and evidence that earned
its disposition. Anonymous synthesis and unsupported conclusions fail this
step even when the prose reads smoothly.

## Choose the decision rule for the kind of disagreement

Use evidential weight rather than votes when the claims are truth-apt, relevant
information is unevenly distributed, competence differs materially, or the
quality of evidence can be compared. Check the claimed expertise and its
basis; never defer automatically to a role label or confident contributor.

Let the accountable decision owner judge trade-offs where several courses are
evidentially defensible but consequences or priorities differ. Consensus can
be useful for genuinely preference-based choices and as a warning that a
proposal is fragile, but agreement is evidence about the group, not proof of
the underlying claim.

Do not use consensus to override unique relevant evidence, an independently
held safety or acceptance judgment, or a decision right that must remain with
its accountable owner. Do not split the difference between claims merely to
close the discussion.

Expertise-weighting has a real robustness cost: the same compromise that
dilutes a strong contribution can also dampen a bad one. When a contributor
may be unreliable or compromised, inspect its evidence directly and seek an
independent check. Averaging every claim buys robustness by degrading ordinary
integration as well.

## Keep unresolved disagreement in the deliverable

For a material disagreement, record the competing claims, the shared facts,
the exact point of divergence, the practical consequence, who owns the
decision and what evidence or event would reopen it. If the owner has authority
and delay is not warranted, the work may proceed over dissent with the reason
recorded. The dissent remains linked; it is not rewritten as agreement.

Escalate when the disagreement concerns purpose, authority, acceptance or a
consequence outside the decision owner's remit. Careful contributors reaching
different judgments is not by itself a reason to escalate.

## Verify the integrated artifact

Check the finished artifact against its purpose and acceptance criteria. Trace
its material conclusions to the ledger, look for omitted conditions and
contradictory claims, and inspect dependencies and seams between contributions.
Confirm that every unresolved item with a material consequence is visible to
the decision owner.

Smooth merging, unanimous contributor reports and a completed ledger are
process observations. None establishes that the integrated result is correct
or useful.

## What this method rests on

The measurements behind this guidance concern finite tasks, equal-status model
teams and mostly knowable answers. They show expert dilution, loss of uniquely
held facts and artifact-level coordination failures; governance sources supply
precedents for named ownership and preserved independent judgment. No source
tests this complete owner-and-claim-ledger method in a continuing team.

The evidence, contrary result and transfer limits are in
[the supporting synthesis](../../knowledge/integrating-expertise-and-disagreement.md).
This skill is draft, has not been tested in a behavioural case and does not
show that a team following it produces a better result.
