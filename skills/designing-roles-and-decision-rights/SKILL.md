# Design roles and decision rights

Shape the organization around decisions the work actually requires. A role is a
continuing cost in context, coordination and maintenance; create one only when a
recurring body of work needs distinct judgment, durable ownership or independence.

## Choose the lightest execution mode that preserves the result

Start from the current operating direction and name the result or judgment now
missing. Then choose among these modes:

- Keep one owner when the work is tightly coupled, the required context is
  shared, and a capable single attempt meets the bar.
- Add a tool or deterministic workflow when the gap is repeatable execution,
  retrieval, validation or state movement rather than judgment.
- Use temporary assistance for a bounded, separable surge, outside expertise or
  independent check that has a clear stopping point.
- Create a persistent role only when the need recurs and someone must retain
  specialized judgment, accountability, relationships or an independent right
  across work cycles.

Do not infer that another agent helps merely because there is more work. Controlled
bounded-task evidence finds gains and losses depend on task structure, not agent
count, and one deployed research system reports a large token premium. Read
[when work earns a role](../../knowledge/shaping-roles-and-decision-rights.md)
before carrying its measurements into a decision.

## Write the role contract before installing the role

State, in one inspectable record:

1. the role's purpose and the result it owns;
2. decisions it may make, decisions it must challenge or escalate, and decisions
   reserved elsewhere;
3. inputs, provenance and access it needs;
4. outputs, consumers and interfaces with other owners;
5. resources and boundaries inherited from the organization;
6. signals for review, combination, succession and retirement.

Give the role enough authority to discharge its responsibility, but never treat
a role definition as new authority for the organization. Keep one owner
accountable for the integrated result even when another role has an independent
challenge, review or veto right.

## Preserve expertise and independence deliberately

Do not use free discussion or consensus as the default interface. In measured
self-organizing LLM teams, integration repeatedly averaged away the strongest
member's knowledge, and naming the expert was not enough. Route each specialist's
evidence to the accountable decision owner; require dissent and its basis to
survive. Where the consequence warrants independence, separate production from
review and state exactly what the reviewer may block or approve.

This is reasoned transfer from bounded agent studies and human governance
frameworks, not a tested recipe for persistent agent organizations.

## Change the organization without dropping the work

Review a role when its work, required expertise, interface load or authority
boundary changes. Add a role when the same material judgment is repeatedly
unowned or independence is repeatedly compromised. Combine roles when their
distinction no longer changes decisions and independence is unnecessary. Retire
a role when its work ends or another mechanism demonstrably supplies it.

Before any change, trace its active commitments, records, permissions, external
relationships and pending decisions. Name the successor or termination for each,
update interfaces, and verify that consumers now know where to go. Use
[coordinating delegated work](../coordinating-delegated-work/SKILL.md) only after
a bounded assignment and its owner exist.

## What this method rests on

The evidence combines controlled bounded-task comparisons, one first-party
multi-agent deployment, one study of unstructured teams, and NIST/NASA governance
descriptions. It does not measure this combined procedure, persistent agent roles
or indefinite operation. The add/combine/retire triggers above are explicit
reasoned guidance, not experimentally established thresholds.
