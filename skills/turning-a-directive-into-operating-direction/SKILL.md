# Turn a continuing directive into operating direction

Keep the directive as the enduring authority. Build beneath it a current,
revisable operating direction that lets the team choose and reassess work. Do
not silently convert your preferred strategy into the directive itself.

## Establish the direction

Write one compact direction record containing:

- **Beneficiaries:** who should be better off, including materially different
  groups whose needs or interests might be hidden by an average.
- **Value hypothesis:** the change that would matter to them and why the
  team's contribution could produce it. Keep activity and outputs separate
  from that outcome.
- **Bounds:** supplied constraints, authority limits, unacceptable harms,
  resource exposure and commitments that a strategy may not trade away.
- **Observable outcomes:** external or independently checkable signs of value,
  intermediate movement and adverse effects. Name what each observation can
  and cannot establish.
- **Strategic assumptions:** the causal steps from action to outcome, the
  important context each requires, the evidence behind it and a plausible way
  it could fail. Mark inference as inference.
- **Review conditions:** the next decision point, evidence needed then and
  earlier events that reopen the direction.

Use a causal map only as far as it clarifies decisions. For uncertain or
exploratory work, a bounded learning hypothesis—what must be learned and which
decision it will change—is more honest than a detailed invented chain.

Stress-test the record with affected people or evidence representing their
different perspectives. Look specifically for a beneficiary omitted by the
chosen framing, an outcome that rewards activity without value, an adverse
effect, and an alternative route that would make the present strategy
unnecessary. Consultation informs direction; it does not transfer the
principal's authority or manufacture consensus.

## Make review decision-led

Choose cadence from the decision, not a universal calendar. Review before
exposure becomes hard to reverse and after enough evidence can exist to change
a choice. Also reopen the direction when a key assumption fails, beneficiary
conditions change, a material harm appears, the principal changes intent, or
a credible alternative changes the opportunity cost.

Prepare evidence before the review. Compare observed outcomes and harms with
the hypotheses and bounds; examine missing or selectively collected data;
then decide whether to retain, revise, replace or stop the current route.
Record the decision, evidence, remaining disagreement and next review trigger.
A persuasive account, completed work or fidelity to an old plan is not by
itself evidence of value.

## Propagate a change

Announcing new direction does not update work already running. For every
material change:

1. Record what changed, why, the evidence or authority behind it, and which
   earlier assumption or choice it supersedes.
2. Trace the change through active commitments, selection and acceptance
   criteria, roles and decision rights, loaded or retrievable context,
   resource allocations, and external promises or interfaces.
3. Reissue, revise or cancel each affected item through its owning mechanism.
   Preserve unaffected work instead of rebuilding everything.
4. Verify that owners and systems received the new basis and no live artifact
   still directs action from a superseded premise.
5. Retain the former direction and disposition so later agents can distinguish
   a deliberate change from drift.

Once direction is established, use [choosing what to work on
next](../choosing-what-to-work-on-next/SKILL.md) to compare bounded increments.
That skill does not decide the beneficiaries, value or strategic assumptions
on which its comparison rests.

## Apply the distinctions to the mission

For accessible work tools, voluntary use and reduced task friction can test a
product hypothesis; shipped features and accessibility claims cannot substitute
for beneficiary evidence. Review after research or release increments and
before continued support costs become hard to unwind.

For community decision work, the direction must preserve conflicting resident
interests. Accurate understanding, missing interests entering the record, and
traceable effects on decisions matter; message volume and apparent consensus
can be adverse signals. An irreversible public decision is an event trigger
even when the normal review date is later.
