# How to assess progress under a continuing mission

Treat the report as an index into evidence, not as the evidence. Reconstruct
the period from the prior directive and causal account, dated decisions,
versioned artifacts, independent observations and measures chosen before
results were visible. Then ask:

1. What changed for beneficiaries? Separate inputs and activity from outputs,
   outcomes, adverse effects and unintended effects.
2. What credible contribution did the organization make? Test its causal
   account and alternatives; contribution is not counterfactual proof.
3. What uncertainty was retired, and what decision changed because of it? A
   changed decision counts as learning only when the evidence warrants it.
4. What is now at risk? Require the next commitment to name an observable
   result, horizon, resource exposure and response if the expectation fails.

Cite the record behind each judgment, preserve conflicts and missing evidence,
and decide whether to continue, redirect, pause or stop. Do not collapse the
judgment into universal weights: revenue, voluntary use, institutional change,
reproducibility, personal judgment and corpus trustworthiness are different.

Loss or absent product-market fit may still buy a proportionate,
decision-relevant update; failure alone does not. A pivot traces to new
evidence or changed context and updates the next test; drift does not. Outputs
are not outcomes, activity is not delivery, and measures invented after seeing
results are exploratory. A long horizon remains credible only when
intermediate work retires uncertainty and makes the next commitment more
falsifiable; renewed promises without new information are adverse evidence.

Keep only records needed to distinguish material alternatives: the current
directive and causal account; a dated decision log; versioned artifacts with
independent observations; and a compact ledger of pre-result measures or
baselines, resource use, results and next expectations. Records can be curated
or falsified, and the lens creates incentives for tidy low-risk narratives.
Independent observations and attention to omitted alternatives reduce that
risk but do not remove it.

A concrete application is in [choosing the next accessible-work
increment](choosing-an-accessible-work-increment.md). Neither source review
nor that transfer demonstrates that the lens improves an organization.
