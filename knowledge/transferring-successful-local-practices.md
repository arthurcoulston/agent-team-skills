# What supports transferring a successful local practice

Structured comparison is better supported than copying a successful case's
surface form. In three small negotiation experiments, learners who compared
two examples were more likely to articulate and later use their common
principle than learners who studied the same examples separately. This
supports extracting a relation across cases; it does not establish a universal
number of examples or an organizational procedure.

Transfer has several independent dimensions. Domain, physical and temporal
setting, function, social setting, and response mode can each move by different
amounts, so “far transfer” without those particulars is not interpretable.

Organizational replication adds recipient and relationship conditions. A
study of 122 internal transfers associated difficulty especially with causal
ambiguity, recipient absorptive capacity, and the source-recipient relationship.
Its retrospective perceptual measures support risks, not causal effect sizes.
Implementation research separates a practice's core from adaptable elements,
while warning that local variation may be warranted adaptation or drift.

Agent studies supply contrary cases. Curator-verified procedures improved
every reported EvoAgentBench cell, while automatic memory methods had negative
cells. Other skill studies found harmful results, excess context cost, and weak
ranking transfer across models. Encoding, routing, and uptake can each break a
genuinely useful source practice.

The promotion ladder is a synthesis: reconstruct the causal claim, compare
cases, state the invariant mechanism and defeat conditions, test the next
boundary against a matched baseline, and promote only to the altitude reached.
One pass supports only the tested recipient, task class, model, harness, and
time. Distribution is not corroboration, and clear instructions cannot repair
an unsupported causal story.
