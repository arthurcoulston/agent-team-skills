# Outline — the territory, by occasion

A provisional map of what this collection has to cover, and what it does not
yet know. It is organised by **occasion**: the moments a founding or leading
agent faces, from the first reading of a directive onward. Skills are found by
the moment they apply to, so the outline is shaped by moments; the concepts
behind them are one lens over it, not its spine.

The starting source is a ratified ledger of what one long-horizon agent
organisation learned, written as named handles. Handle names below are quoted
as ratified. A handle listed under an occasion is a candidate for that
occasion's guidance, not settled coverage; several bear on more than one
moment, and that is expected.

Everything here is revisable. An occasion may split, merge, or turn out to be
the wrong cut. Open questions are listed where they arise.

## The reader

An agent that wakes with a directive and this collection, and has to build and
lead a team of agents with minimal help from human experts and minimal
scaffolding. It should not have to relearn foundational lessons. Better
scaffolding would make it faster; the guidance must not depend on any.

## Occasions

### 1. Reading the directive and deciding what the team is for

The first moment. Separate the mission, the team that pursues it, and the work
the team produces; a team whose apparatus names its mission cannot serve
another. Decide what the team's product is and what "enough" means before
anything is built.

Draws on: Mission-Free Apparatus, The Machine Is the Product, Apparatus /
Workspace / Product, Definition of Enough, Output Is Scaffolding.

Open: the ledger's source treats strategy (choosing what to invest in next) as
its own layer between mission and work. Whether a founding agent needs that
layer on day one, or only once there is something to choose between, is
unsettled.

### 2. Standing up the first seats

Deciding which roles exist, what each may do, and what each loads. A seat's
context envelope is a design decision on a par with its tools and authority.
Building and reviewing are separated; designing and building are not. A
subagent is an instrument, never a seat.

Draws on: The Envelope, Graph Engineering, Build ≠ Review, Design+Build Fuse,
Instrument, Not a Role, Envelope by Enumeration.

Open: how many seats a founder should start with, and which one it should be
itself. The source's answer is fractal (a leader, one product, its workers, at
every scope); whether that transfers to a team with no human operator is a
question for evidence.

### 3. Writing what an agent loads

Authoring standing instructions, skill entrypoints, indexed references and
searchable knowledge, and deciding which of those a given line belongs in.
An agent knows only what is in its context; what is left out matters as much
as what is put in. Name things the model already knows rather than describing
qualities. Write the question rather than the answer where an example would
harden into a rule.

Draws on: Context Is the World, The Envelope, Exemplars, Write the Question,
Not the Answer, Truncation Is Silent, Brown M&M.

This is the first exemplar and the pilot's first item. The collection uses
this guidance on itself.

### 4. Turning direction into work

Decomposing a mission into bounded, ready work with a visible state; keeping
unready work out of the queue; making sure a decision is written into the
system and not only agreed; putting finished work where its reader looks.

Draws on: Ready vs Held, Ratified ≠ In Force, Landing Surface, Only
Conclusions Survive.

Open: the source's work vocabulary (mission, strategy, initiative, version,
instance) is one team's; the collection needs to say what is general in it.

### 5. Deciding when to stop, and when not to start

No agent asks whether it has done enough. Stopping is infrastructure: wake
on work, sleep otherwise, cap retries, and never let the fixer raise the cap.
Distinguish stopped, held, blocked, repairing and idle.

Draws on: They Never Stop, Wake-on-Change, Definition of Enough, Treadmill
Brake, Ready vs Held, When It Asks to Stop, Stop.

This is the second exemplar: a judgment call rather than an authoring task,
chosen so the entry form is tested by both shapes.

### 6. Keeping the team honest

Checking that context is clean before work starts; knowing that the harness
will recruit an agent against the team's own rules; reading the source rather
than trusting notes about it; believing side effects rather than reports;
logging whether a check could read its input; treating a half-read file as
unread.

Draws on: Integrity Gate, Brown M&M, Harness Recruitment, Apparatus Beats
Harness, Capability-Closed ≠ Context-Closed, Re-verify on Upgrade, Truncation
Is Silent, Belief Is a Cache, Side Effects, Not Narration, read=ok, One
Sentinel, One Proposition, Trace Beats Record, Silent Failure.

Open: this is the largest cluster and probably more than one occasion. A
likely split is "before a session starts" from "when a record and reality
disagree".

### 7. Recovering when something breaks

Fixing low first and escalating in steps, with the human last; fixing the
machine rather than the output; stopping when the agent asks to.

Draws on: Self-Healing Ladder, Treadmill Brake, Fix the Machine, Not the
Output, When It Asks to Stop, Stop.

### 8. Working with the human

Humans decide in conversation and agents make every file change. Ask in
prose, never menus. A human's words land harder than meant. If nothing was
filed, the human saw nothing. Evaluate the team, not each output. Trust a
message according to its channel, and build no channel outsiders can use to
trigger work.

Draws on: Humans Direct; Agents Write, Prose, Not Menus, Operator Weight,
Absence Isn't Awareness, Evaluate the Team, Not the Output, Messaging Blast
Radius, No Inbound Channel, The Human Bottleneck.

Open: the reader may have no human expert at hand. Which of these still apply
when the only human is an occasional effector, and which assume a present
operator, needs to be marked entry by entry.

### 9. Calibrating the bar

Agents set the bar higher than the use needs. Measure a failure before
building machinery against it. Sensible gates add up to a team that cannot
ship. A ratification gate is calibration, not policy, and removing one is an
audit.

Draws on: Rigor Creep, Audit the Fear, Permission Creep, Gates Are Temporary.

### 10. Keeping the machine general, and learning from its own work

The apparatus should work on any comparable model. Today's output exists to
make tomorrow's machine stronger. Leaders edit their own context while using
it, which needs its own discipline.

Draws on: The Model Is a Service, Fix the Machine, Not the Output, Output Is
Scaffolding, Self-Surgery, Envelope by Enumeration.

## Not yet covered

Things a founding agent will face that the starting source says little
about. Each is a research question, not a gap to fill from memory.

- Choosing the work: how a team selects what to invest in next, and when a
  research thread should stop.
- Growing from one seat to many: when to add a seat, when to split one, and
  how a handoff between sessions carries state without a conversation.
- Capacity and spend: how a team sizes a batch, holds a budget, and reports
  what it cost.
- Evaluating the team's own output when no human will read most of it.
- Which of the above changes when the team has no operator at all.

## Lenses

The collection is one set of records drawn in more than one way. Each lens is
a view over the same entries and relations; a lens that cannot be drawn from
recorded relations is the signal that a relation is missing.

- **Concept.** The ledger's own map: handles in their ratified clusters and
  order. Drawn from the entries' handle names; needs no new relation.
- **Occasion.** This outline: which entries serve which moment. Needs a
  relation from an entry to an occasion, which the layout does not yet have.
- **Context flow.** Which guidance reaches which seat, and by what route
  (always loaded, loaded on occasion, looked up). Needs a relation naming a
  seat kind and a loading mode.
- **Directive decomposition.** How a mission becomes work, roles and context,
  and how learning changes what later agents receive. Needs an ordering
  relation between occasions, or a separate small set of records.

Which of these earn a fixed relation is decided by prototyping them over the
two exemplars, with the people who will read the maps in the room.
