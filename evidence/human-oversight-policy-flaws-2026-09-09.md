# Reading — The Flaws of Policies Requiring Human Oversight of Government Algorithms

[Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05067v4). Ben Green. v1 2021-09-10; v4
2022-10-24. Published in *Computer Law & Security Review*, volume 45, 2022.
Reading scope: the abstract and metadata only — see the caveat below.

## What it argues

Quoted from the v4 abstract:

> "Despite the widespread turn to human oversight, these policies rest on an
> uninterrogated assumption: that people are able to effectively oversee
> algorithmic decision-making. In this article, I survey 41 policies that
> prescribe human oversight of government algorithms and find that they suffer
> from two significant flaws. First, evidence suggests that people are unable to
> perform the desired oversight functions. Second, as a result of the first
> flaw, human oversight policies legitimize government uses of faulty and
> controversial algorithms without addressing the fundamental issues with these
> tools. Thus, rather than protect against the potential harms of algorithmic
> decision-making in government, human oversight policies provide a false sense
> of security in adopting algorithms and enable vendors and agencies to shirk
> accountability for algorithmic harms."

The proposed remedy is institutional rather than individual: agencies "must
justify that it is appropriate to incorporate an algorithm into decision-making
and that any proposed forms of human oversight are supported by empirical
evidence", and those justifications "must receive democratic public review and
approval before the agency can adopt the algorithm."

## Scope

Government algorithmic decision systems and the law and policy governing them —
risk assessment, benefits determination and similar — not language-model agents.
The transfer to an agent team asking a person for a decision is by analogy: the
person in the loop is a finite, fallible instrument, and requiring their
approval is not the same as obtaining their judgment.

This is a reading of an abstract with quoted material, not a reading of the
article.
