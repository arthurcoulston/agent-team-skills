# Reading — financing entrepreneurial experimentation

[Nanda and Rhodes-Kropf](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/684983)
explain why staged investment has value under uncertainty: a bounded initial
commitment can produce information that rationally changes whether to
continue or abandon. Failure itself is not progress, and experiment cost,
stage and financing conditions change what evidence is informative.

This supports treating early progress as a decision-relevant update rather
than profit or product-market fit alone. The article concerns venture-financed
technology and investor returns; it does not establish how to value public
goods, donor outcomes or an agent team's report.
