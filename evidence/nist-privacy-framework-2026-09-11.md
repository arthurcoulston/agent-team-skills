# Reading — NIST Privacy Framework 1.0

The voluntary, technology- and jurisdiction-agnostic framework calls for
communicating data-processing purposes and practices, enabling individuals'
preferences and requests, carrying processing permissions with data, aligning
data and system lifecycles, minimizing logged data, and testing technical
measures. This supports making purpose, permitted use, disclosure, retention,
and preferences part of an interface contract rather than treating secure
transport as sufficient privacy management.

The framework is risk- and outcome-based, not a law or a universal consent
schema. It does not determine which legal basis, assurance level, retention
period, or notice a particular interface requires.
